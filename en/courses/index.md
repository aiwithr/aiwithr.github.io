## Why Learn Artificial Intelligence?

1. One technology is quietly surrounding us in every sphere of life: Artificial Intelligence. Put simply, technology is being taught to make decisions just like human beings — to serve human needs. In healthcare, it helps provide patients with proper medical attention and minimizes human error. Furthermore, instead of testing experimental treatments directly on patients, AI simulations are transforming 'drug discovery'. Today, AI's pivotal contribution to COVID-19 drug discovery is widely recognized; without it, the research timeline would have stretched far longer. Many nations deployed this technology to control and track the pandemic.

2. Governments in developed countries leverage artificial intelligence for urban planning, mass transit optimization, modifying river courses, early flood warning, equitable distribution of public resources, forecasting future pension obligations, predictive policing, and city-wide intelligent traffic management — among thousands of applications. Beyond war gaming, the strategic adoption of AI by the armed forces emerged in the United States in 2014 under the 'Third Offset Strategy'. In 2018, the Pentagon outlined a \$2 billion plan dedicated to artificial intelligence applications.

3. The reality is that even if we do not consciously perceive how it unfolds, our surroundings are undergoing profound transformation driven by artificial intelligence. Whether we welcome it or not, it has seamlessly integrated into the fabric of daily life. From healthcare to public administration, transportation to education — whatever people strive to accomplish, AI empowers and simplifies. Truly, unprecedented capability is being placed into human hands, yet very few currently realize its magnitude. The genesis of artificial intelligence begins with teaching the machine — and that is precisely where machine learning comes in. In other words, machine learning is the core process that endows machines with intelligence.

4. A global movement around *AI for Social Good* is actively harnessing data to serve humanity. We possess massive volumes of data, but at this stage, we often fail to extract meaningful wisdom from it. The methodologies for turning raw data into wisdom will be demonstrated throughout this training. Understanding machine learning is essential because its impact now touches every sector. Artificial intelligence is both a present reality and an indispensable future skillset.

## How Will We Learn? Solving a Real-World Problem

Understanding the underlying philosophy of machine learning makes the learning curve remarkably smooth. You will know where you started, where you need to go, and exactly where you stand right now. Colleagues and friends across the global machine learning community always share one golden piece of advice: if you want to learn machine learning, start with [Project Titanic](http://kaggle.com/c/titanic). *This is the ultimate gateway to machine learning.* Period. When you understand the problem clearly, the solution lies well within reach.

## Learning Outcome: The Titanic Challenge

The year was 1912. A true historical event. We have James Cameron to thank for making the story unforgettable. The 'unsinkable' RMS Titanic sank while crossing the Atlantic on its maiden voyage after colliding with an iceberg. It was 11:40 at night. We can envision that among the 2,224 passengers and crew, almost everyone had retired to sleep in their cabins or bunks. What followed is etched in history.

1,502 people perished on that voyage.

This timeless data science challenge was crafted around this real historical tragedy. Here, we are provided with two datasets. The first dataset contains 12 attributes describing 891 individuals, including a specific column indicating whether each passenger survived or died. Notice the nature of this data: authentic historical records. Their names and their documented fates are preserved across the internet. Nothing here is fabricated.

Now comes the challenge: we are given a second dataset containing 418 new individuals outside the original 891. Our mission is to determine their fates. There are 418 rows containing every single attribute except that one crucial column indicating whether they lived or died. We do not know what happened to them.

## The Challenge: Predict Who Survived

So, what is the Titanic Challenge?
{: .notice}

From the dataset of those 418 individuals, we must build a model to predict who survived and who perished. When I first encountered this problem, I was genuinely amazed. How is this possible? How could anyone know? Predicting human survival seems beyond our capability. How could a machine learning algorithm accomplish this?
